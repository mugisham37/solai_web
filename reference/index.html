The responsiveness question

A phone layout stretched to 1400px isn't a desktop design.

Mobile-first was right — most sellers will never open this on anything else. But a seller doing her books on a borrowed laptop, and a support agent working a dispute queue all day, are different people with different jobs. So the rule isn't "make it fit". It's: every breakpoint has to earn its extra width by showing something the narrower one had to hide.

Phone · under 700px

One column, thumb-reachable actions pinned to the bottom, bottom tab bar, sheets that slide up from the edge. This is the reference design and nothing is cut from it.

Tablet · 700–1000px

Content stops stretching and centres at a readable measure; cards go two-up; the phone status bar becomes a browser bar; sheets become centred dialogs. The extra width buys density, not longer lines.

Desktop · 1000px and up

Two columns with a sticky context rail, tab bar becomes a left sidebar, tables stop being cards and become tables. Editing and its preview are visible at the same time.

Built with container queries, not viewport queries

Layouts respond to the width of the frame they're in, not the width of the browser window. Two things follow. First, the device switcher is honest — resizing the frame triggers exactly the rules a real device would. Second, a component keeps its behaviour when it moves: the money-flow strip is the same code on a phone, in a desktop sidebar and inside an admin case.

Cost: about 40 lines of CSS and no JavaScript. There is no separate desktop build and no device sniffing.

Three stakeholders

The third one is the reason the other two trust us.

SolAI holds other people's money. That means the promises on the buyer's screen — held funds, a 48-hour dispute answer, a refund if nothing arrives — are only real if somebody can actually keep them. The admin console isn't back-office tooling bolted on at the end; it's where the product's central promise is either honoured or broken.

Seller · screens 1–12

Wants to be paid, quickly, without paperwork. Mobile-first, five screens to live, plans only after a payout. Needs to know why money is held and exactly when it moves.

Buyer · screens 13–15

Wants to not be robbed. Sees the seller's real record, a mobile-money-first checkout, held funds and a delivery code that only they hold. One tap to report a problem.

Admin · screens A1–A6

Wants to settle a case correctly in under 48 hours with evidence in front of them, stop the fraudulent accounts, and prove at any moment that the ledger matches the bank.

The one rule the console is built around

An agent chooses an outcome. They never choose an amount and they never choose a destination.

Both are already fixed by the order and by the wallet the buyer paid from. There is no "send funds" field anywhere in the product, no free-text destination, and no way to release to an account that isn't already on the order. That single constraint removes the obvious way an insider steals, and it's why the seller's file is read-only even though it's covered in controls.

Roles. Support resolves and moderates. Finance approves anything above RWF 100,000 and can unmask a wallet or an ID, with a second reviewer. Only an admin suspends an account or changes hold rules. Nobody at any level can move money to an arbitrary destination.

Everything is logged. Actor, role, reason code, ledger reference, and — for unmasking — who approved it. The log is append-only; a correction is a new entry, never an edit.

Suspension never eats a buyer's money. Blocking a seller stops new orders. Funds already held are still resolved case by case under the normal rules.

The rule behind every cut

Anything asked for before a seller can receive their first payment costs us people. Anything offered after a small win converts, because by then they're the one asking.

Nothing was deleted from the product. Plans, ad formats, Meta, community and events all still exist — they moved behind the moment that earns the right to ask. The admin console is the one thing added rather than moved, because the escrow promise was previously unenforceable.

Honest about what this is

Original throughout. Every word, icon and colour was made for SolAI.

Flow, not pixels. Screens 6–15 and the whole console are a designed proposal, clearly a proposal.

No functionality. Generation is timing, ledgers are static, nothing submits and no money exists.

Translations need a native pass. The RW / SW / FR strings prove the plumbing; they are not final copy.

Container queries need a 2023-or-newer browser. Older ones get the phone layout at every width, which is a safe floor rather than a broken page.
